The contract behind cykablyat.vip

The borrow screen is the first page of the site and it is wired to CBLending on Robinhood Chain. It reads the chain with no wallet attached, and it signs when one is connected. This is what it is pointed at, what it assumes, what must not be changed, and how it gets switched off.

1. What it is pointed at

All four live in src/lib/chain.ts and src/lib/rpc.ts. The proxy address is the one to call — never the implementation, whose storage is empty by design.

The lent token now answers symbol() with CB (“cyka blyat”), matching the $CB on the page. It did not always: the deployment before this one said WN. The page’s copy is BORROWED in src/components/protocol/tokens.ts and what the chain reports is Market.cbSymbol — kept apart on purpose, so the next disagreement shows up in code rather than in a wallet.

The ABI is not the compiled artifact. It is hand-written and as const in src/lib/abi.ts, which is what lets viem type the arguments — and it carries every custom error, because a revert can only be turned into a sentence if its error is in the ABI it was given.

2. What the page already assumes

These are compiled into it. If the deployment disagrees with any of them, the page is wrong and it needs to be corrected before it is switched on — not after.

AssumptionValueWhere
MAX_LTV80% — the most you may borrow tosrc/lib/health.ts
LIQ_LTV90% — the point of liquidationsrc/lib/health.ts
LiquidationAll of it. No partial, no grace.stated on the page
Ruler scale0 → 100, alwaysrulerPct
UI cap79%, not 80 — see belowUI_MAX_LTV

Two of those deserve a sentence each

The ruler is drawn 0 → 100 everywhere. The 80 and 90 marks therefore land in the same place on the borrow form and on every position, which is the whole reason several loans can be compared at a glance. Scaling to the liquidation threshold instead — ltv / 90 * 100 — puts 80% at 89% of the bar and silently destroys that.

The page stops at 79%, not 80%. The contract rounds three ways in its own favour: collateral value down, debt value up, and the final ratio up. A loan computed at exactly 80% therefore arrives on-chain as 8001 basis points and is refused. Everything offered as a maximum is shaded below the limit first, so "the most you can borrow" is a number that actually goes through.

3. Three behaviours that must not be changed

All three are implemented and correct. They are written down because all three are easy to "tidy up" into something wrong.

Adding collateral and repaying are never blocked

The 80% cap applies to borrowing and to withdrawing collateral. It does not apply to adding collateral or to repaying — those two only ever improve a position, so they are allowed at any ratio, including when the position is already past 80%. Gating all four together looks tidy and breaks the one action that saves someone who is about to be liquidated. The four buttons live in src/components/protocol/Positions.tsx and none of them is disabled on health.

Every write simulates before it signs

simulateContract runs the call against current state and throws the contract's own error, which describeError turns into a sentence — so "that works out at 80.4%, past the 80% limit" appears before the wallet popup rather than as a failed transaction somebody already paid gas for. Do not replace it with a client-side guess at the limit: that would be a second risk rule beside healthOf(), which is the next thing on this list.

One risk scale, not three

Health comes from healthOf() and nothing else: under 80 healthy, 80–90 at risk, 90 and over liquidatable. Adding a second rule-of-thumb alongside it produces rows that are red next to a button that says everything is fine. The words are rendered too, not just the colour, so the state does not depend on being able to see red.

4. Switching it off, and the one line to never widen

LIVE in src/components/ProtocolSection.tsx is a real kill switch. Set it to false and the wallet chip, the confirm button and every path that can send a transaction disappear, along with every sentence claiming one can be sent; the screen keeps reading the chain and stays honest about being inert. It used to change two strings and leave a "not live yet" footer under a "Review borrow" button — if you are tempted to make it cheaper again, that is what cheaper looked like.

The policy is written twice, and the browser enforces the intersection: connect-src in src/app/layout.tsx (built from RPC_ORIGIN) and in public/_headers (plain text, which no build step touches). Widen one alone and every local check passes while the deployed site cannot reach the chain. npm run check:csp compares them and fails on drift.

not this connect-src 'self' * — or default-src widened, or a wildcard host.

this connect-src 'self' https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com — named origins only.

That single line is what keeps "a script on this page cannot ask for your wallet" a fact rather than a hope. Widening it to everything removes the protection entirely while looking like a one-word change. The other half is frame-ancestors 'none', which only public/_headers can carry — it is what stops this signing UI being framed.
